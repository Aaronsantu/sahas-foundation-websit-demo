Our Grassroots Action

SAHAS Initiative as Ni-Kshay Mitra

SAHAS registered as a Ni-Kshay Mitra partner in 2023. With funds raised from Trustees, SAHAS adopted 10 poor and needy TB patients for a year starting in March 2024.

As the world celebrated ‘World TB Day’ on March 24, SAHAS distributed WHO-approved dry nutritious food packets to these 10 adopted patients. Over the course of 12 months, these same nutritious food packets continue to be supplied regularly.

The health progress of all 10 patients is actively monitored on the dedicated portal provided by the Government.

Understanding Tuberculosis

A layman's understanding of TB reveals crucial realities about how the disease spreads and impacts lives:

  • The bacteria is more or less omnipresent in our surroundings.
  • A majority of individuals are infected without showing immediate symptoms.
  • The disease manifests primarily when immune systems become severely compromised.
  • In India, malnutrition remains a primary driver of lowered immunity.
  • Globally, HIV patients are particularly susceptible.

While poverty remains a primary culprit, TB has increasingly breached the class barrier. This is due to a general lowering of immunity stemming from modern urban living habits, including poor dietary choices.

Key Facts About TB

(a) Highly Infectious & Air-Transmitted

Pulmonary TB is highly infectious. People with active TB can infect 5 to 15 people through close contact over the course of a year. The disease spreads through the air when an infected person coughs or speaks. When breathed in, bacteria can lie dormant (latent TB infection) or develop into full-blown active disease.

(b) Beyond Pulmonary Complications

Pulmonary TB—affecting the lungs—is not the only type, and each form presents its own complications. The TB bacteria is extremely tenacious and resists treatment, requiring a long and torturous multi-drug regimen lasting several months. Missing even a single dose when feeling better can cause the bacteria to adapt and mutate, leaving no option other than much harsher medication.

Drug-Resistant Tuberculosis (DR-TB)

The rise in cases and deaths due to drug-resistant tuberculosis poses a major challenge to India's ambition to end the disease by 2025.

According to the World Health Organization (WHO), India’s count of 119,000 cases makes up over 26% of global MDR-TB cases.

“DR-TB is of growing concern in India,” acknowledges C. Padmapriyadarsini, Director of the ICMR-NIRT in Chennai, highlighting drivers such as irregular treatment, delayed diagnosis, and premature discontinuation of medication.

  • Mutation: TB bacteria mutate into Multi-Drug-Resistant (MDR-TB) and Extensively Drug-Resistant (XDR-TB) strains due to treatment misuse and interruption.
  • Resistant Strains: MDR-TB is resistant to isoniazid and rifampicin. XDR-TB is resistant to rifampicin, fluoroquinolones, and injectable second-line drugs.
  • New Treatments: DR-TB is now being treated with newer oral drugs like bedaquiline and delamanid, available free at government and designated hospitals, offering higher effectiveness with fewer side effects.

Stigma & Crippling Expenses

Social stigma prevents early diagnosis. Though TB now crosses class lines, “TB remains a disease of the poor,” notes Blessina Kumar, CEO of the Global Coalition of TB Advocates. Many patients refrain from seeking treatment out of fear or lack of support.

Financial Burden: DR-TB treatment can take up to two years, compared to six months for regular TB. Stopping treatment midway due to financial strain leads to drug resistance, which is why programs like Directly Observed Therapy (DOT) and Ni-kshay Mitra are critical.

Government Initiatives & Global Praise

The Government of India is heavily focusing on diagnostics and treatment affordability:

  • Free provision of advanced DR-TB drugs (bedaquiline & delamanid).
  • Financial assistance of Rs 500 per month under the Ni-kshay Poshan Yojana along with free medicine distribution.
  • Healthcare access support through Ayushman Bharat (Jan Arogya Yojana).
  • Public-Private partnerships (e.g., Haryana state partnering with Medanta hospital).

Dr. Lucica Ditiu, Executive Director of Stop TB Partnership:
“India is inspiring the world in TB elimination. Every country should have the Ni-kshay Mitra initiative. I salute the way it is happening at sub-national levels too, not in pilot mode but at scale.”
